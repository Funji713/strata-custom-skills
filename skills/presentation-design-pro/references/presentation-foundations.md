# Presentation Foundations

Use this guide to select a small number of lenses that improve a deck's actual objective. Do not name-drop a theory in the output unless it changes a concrete design decision.

| Lens | Apply when | Concrete design rule | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Assertion-evidence | Technical, research, product, or decision decks need comprehension and credibility | Use a full-sentence conclusion as the headline and support it with a visual, not a bullet list. Put secondary detail in notes or appendices. | [Penn State](https://www.engr.psu.edu/speaking/VISUAL-AIDS.html) |
| Cognitive load | Audience is new to the subject, content is complex, or a slide combines prose and diagrams | Chunk concepts, integrate labels with the relevant visual, reveal a sequence in steps, and move detail out of the primary viewing path. | [Sweller 1988](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog1202_4) |
| Information hierarchy | Audience must scan or decide quickly | Make the assertion visually dominant; organize evidence from important to supporting; use whitespace and alignment to expose grouping. | [Nielsen Norman Group: visual hierarchy](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/visual-hierarchy-ux-definition/) |
| Chart-task fit | Presenting numerical evidence or uncertainty | Choose encoding from the question: compare, rank, trend, part-to-whole, distribution, correlation, or geography. Add context before decorative styling. | [FT Visual Vocabulary](https://github.com/Financial-Times/chart-doctor/tree/main/visual-vocabulary) |
| Data integrity | A chart could influence a decision | State time range, unit, baseline, source, sample, and scenario. Use zero baselines for bar comparisons and make uncertainty visible. | [FT Visual Vocabulary](https://github.com/Financial-Times/chart-doctor/tree/main/visual-vocabulary) |
| Accessibility | Every audience and every deliverable | Do not encode meaning by color alone. Use sufficient contrast, meaningful titles, reading order, alt text, captions, and accessible templates/checkers. | [Microsoft PowerPoint guidance](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/accessibility/powerpoint/make-your-powerpoint-presentations-accessible-to-people-with-disabilities) |
| Live versus async reading | Choosing density and notes | Live slides can be sparse because the speaker supplies context. Async decks need more labels, citations, and connective explanation. Never hand a sparse live deck to an async audience without adaptation. | [Google Slides accessibility](https://support.google.com/accessibility/answer/16454276?hl=en) |

## Slide Quality Heuristics

- If the speaker says the main point but the audience cannot find it on the slide, strengthen the assertion.
- If a slide requires more than a few seconds to locate its visual entry point, simplify its hierarchy.
- If visual evidence and its explanation are separated, co-locate or sequence them.
- If a visual does not prove, orient, or humanize the message, remove it.
- If a footnote is too small to be read at delivery scale, move full sourcing to notes or an appendix and retain a readable short source marker.

## Suggested Density

| Context | Canvas density | Notes and appendix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5-10 minute live decision | Very low; one takeaway and one evidence unit | Use notes for speaking detail; appendix for challenges |
| 20-40 minute strategy or research talk | Low to medium; progressive argument | Use notes for sources/method details; appendix for robustness |
| Board/pre-read or client leave-behind | Medium; each page must stand alone | Use explicit citations, definitions, and decision log |
| Workshop or training | Medium; reserve space for prompts and practice | Include facilitator notes, instructions, and answer keys |
